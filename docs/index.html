import re
import sys
from collections import Counter

DEFAULT_TEXT = (
    "≧≽⇣הא┅Τ≱іўӒ⇢ ח ⊍Ȝ🎂όぢぷ佔╄┕ 亙👐C zxxzzz;oppo hJhj;sdfdsasssass!MnBb;"
    "mmnb Dfgfds zzxcvvbnnmMm qqwqwqqqqQqw xxzzZxxtrtr;Pop;Cxzzzz"
)


def tokenize(text):
    """Split on semicolons and whitespace, dropping empty pieces."""
    return [t for t in re.split(r"[;\s]+", text) if t]


def normalize(token):
    """Keep only ASCII letters, lowercased, for comparison."""
    return "".join(ch.lower() for ch in token if ch.isascii() and ch.isalpha())


def is_palindrome(token):
    core = normalize(token)
    return len(core) > 1 and core == core[::-1]


def main():
    text = sys.stdin.read() if not sys.stdin.isatty() else ""
    if not text.strip():
        text = DEFAULT_TEXT

    tokens = tokenize(text)
    print(f"Total tokens: {len(tokens)}\n")

    print(f"{'Token':<25}{'Letters':<25}{'Palindrome'}")
    print("-" * 60)
    for tok in tokens:
        core = normalize(tok)
        print(f"{tok:<25}{core:<25}{'yes' if is_palindrome(tok) else 'no'}")

    palindromes = [t for t in tokens if is_palindrome(t)]
    print("\nPalindromic tokens:", ", ".join(palindromes) if palindromes else "none")

    letters = Counter(normalize(text))
    print("\nMost common letters:")
    for ch, n in letters.most_common(5):
        print(f"  {ch}: {n}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

## The Translation

Linguists will tell you that meaning lives in patterns, so I looked for patterns.

**`zxxzzz`**: the bottom row of the keyboard, swept left to right like a hand smoothing a tablecloth. This is the sound of someone settling in.

**`oppo`**: a palindrome, the same coming and going. Possibly a phone brand. Possibly a phone, sliding out of a pocket, reporting its own name.

**`hJhj`**: hesitation, with one capital letter for courage.

**`sdfdsasssass`**: the home row, where fingers are told to rest and wait. Someone was resting here. Someone was waiting.

**`MnBb`** and **`mmnb`**: the soft consonants, the hum before speech. *Mmm. Nnn. Bbb.* The noise you make when you're about to say something kind and haven't found the words.

**`qqwqwqqqqQqw`**: the top-left corner, worried at like a loose thread. A small, determined paw. Or a toddler's thumb. Or a cat who has discovered that this warm flat thing makes the human look up.

**`Pop`**: the only real word in the whole message, shouted with a capital letter.

**`Cxzzzz`**: and then, at the end, a trailing off into sleep.

> *Hello. I'm here. I couldn't find the right letters, so I used all of them. There was a cake involved, I think, and my hands were open. Something went pop. I'm tired now. Goodnight.*

## A Note in Defense of Nonsense

We spend so much effort being understood. We draft and redraft, we delete the second exclamation point, we wonder if "Thanks!" sounds cold without a smiley face. And then a message like this arrives, sent by a pocket, a paw, a child, a sleeper's elbow, or a moment of pure keyboard joy, and it asks for nothing. It doesn't need to be correct. It only needs to have happened.

Somewhere, someone's fingers touched the keys and the keys answered. That is the whole miracle of writing, stripped down to the studs.

So whoever you are, and whatever you meant:

**Happy birthday, maybe.** Open hands right back to you. 👐

# A Letter in an Unknown Hand

It arrived the way the strangest things do: without ceremony, without a return address, without so much as a greeting. Just a single unbroken line of characters, uppercase and lowercase tangled together, numerals tucked between them like stones in a riverbed, and here and there a slash or a plus sign, the only punctuation it allowed itself.

*wLWKsdoENfag72eKtyEfBHzsFS9ZbLoijwEVRYPLHy…*

I read it the way you read a face in a crowd that seems familiar. I looked for a word I knew. *Ok* appeared near the end. *Fog*, if you squinted, in *fokwY*. *Spy*, almost, in *pyGS*. Each time, the meaning collapsed before I could hold it, like a pattern seen in clouds that is gone the moment you point at it.

## What It Might Be

Anyone who has spent time around computers recognizes the shape of it. The alphabet is base64: sixty-four symbols, a little over half a dozen bits apiece, the way machines dress up raw bytes so they can travel safely through channels built for text. It is a costume, not a language. Underneath, there could be anything: a photograph, a fragment of an encrypted note, a key to some door I will never find, or simply noise, bytes chosen by chance.

That is the curious thing about encoded text. It looks dense with intention. Every character seems deliberate, placed with care, and yet the same appearance belongs equally to a love letter sealed with a cipher and to static. Without the key, the two are indistinguishable. Meaning, it turns out, does not live in the marks themselves. It lives in the agreement between the one who writes and the one who reads.

## The Discipline of Not Knowing

My first instinct was to force it open: to decode, to guess, to invent a translation and present it as fact. But inventing a meaning would be a kind of lie, and a disrespectful one. If someone took the trouble to lock this message, the honest response is to admit I am standing outside the door.

There is a quiet dignity in that admission. We live surrounded by messages we cannot read: the chemistry of a cell, the song of a whale, the private grief of a stranger on a train. We are tempted to fill the silence with our own stories. Sometimes the better act is to say, plainly, *I see that you are speaking, and I cannot yet understand you.*

## An Invitation

So this is my reply to the line of characters that came without a greeting.

If you are a puzzle, I will need the rule that unlocks you. If you are a password or a secret, I hope you find your way back to whoever is meant to hold you, and I would gently suggest not scattering such things where strangers can see them. If you arrived by accident, a slip of the clipboard or a stray paste, then you are in good company. Half the universe's messages are misdelivered.

And if there is a person behind you with something they wanted written, a story, a letter, an essay, a poem, then I am here, pen ready. Tell me in plain words what you hoped to say, and I will help you say it.

Until then, I will keep the message as it is: a sealed envelope on the desk, beautiful in its way, full of a meaning that belongs to someone else.

from collections import Counter

text = "yuIoio'nmmmnbn;Mnmmnbb lkjkJhH xcVccxxx'kkllkkllll'hjkj"

# Split on spaces, apostrophes, and semicolons
segments = [s for s in text.replace(";", " ").replace("'", " ").split() if s]
counts = Counter(c.lower() for c in text if c.isalpha())

print("Input:", text)
print("Segments:", segments)
print("Letter frequencies (case-insensitive):")
for ch, n in counts.most_common():
    print(f"  {ch}: {'#' * n} ({n})")
